<h2>What should I do first after buying 2‑5 acres of raw land?</h2>
<p>If you have raw land, the first five actions save you weeks and lakhs.</p>
<ol>
<li>Run a percolation test on three spots (north, centre, south). Record infiltration in mm/hr.</li>
<li>Map the contour with a laser level or the <a href="https://www.anvitam.com/site-analysis">Site Intelligence & Contour Hydrology Tool</a>. Identify natural flow paths.</li>
<li>Mark the 100‑year flood line using historic monsoon data (Alibaug sees 3,000 mm in a season).</li>
<li>Sketch a keyline layout: swales on the contour, capture ponds on the low points, and a raised platform for the villa.</li>
<li>Invite a local contractor who has built at least one rammed‑earth wall. Ask for a reference on expansive soil handling.</li>
</ol>
<p>These steps happen before you call an excavator. They turn a vague plot into a data‑driven site.</p>
<h2>How do I size swales and living pools to handle a 100 mm rain event?</h2>
<p>A 100 mm storm on a 1,000 sq m roof drops 100,000 L of water. If you let it run off into a swale, the volume you need is:</p>
<p>Swale volume = length × width × depth. I use a 0.8 m depth for Laterite (high infiltration) and 1.5 m for Black Cotton (low infiltration). For a 30 m long, 5 m wide swale on Laterite: 30 × 5 × 0.8 = 120 m³ (120,000 L). That holds the entire storm with a 20 % safety margin.</p>
