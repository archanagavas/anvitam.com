<h2>What is the 18‑Month Roadmap?</h2>
<p>Months 1‑6: Hydrology & Soil (15 % of budget). Conduct 24‑hour jar test, 15 mm sausage test, percolation mapping, and topographic survey. Produce a water‑budget that captures 120 % of design monsoon runoff. Months 7‑12: Foundations & Earth‑walls (35 %). Excavate to 1.5 m in Gujarat black cotton clay, 0.8 m in Goa laterite, lay 300 mm rammed‑earth footings stabilized with 8 % lime‑pozzolan, and erect 300 mm thick walls. Months 13‑15: Roof, Openings & Passive‑Cooling (25 %). Install 1.8 m overhangs, ventilated double‑skin roof, and 150 mm insulated earth‑floor with thermal‑mass calculations showing a 4‑6 °C temperature drop. Months 16‑18: Finishes, Landscape & Permaculture (25 %). Apply breathable lime‑plaster, plant native Casuarina windbreaks, dig swales lined with laterite stone, and install rain‑water harvesting tanks sized at 1.2 m³ per kW of roof area. Total timeline 18 months, total cost $150 k‑$500 k depending on site conditions, with a 4 % contingency built into each phase.</p>

Follow the steps exactly and you avoid costly redesigns, water‑logging during the first monsoon, and premature wall cracking.</p>

<h2>How Does Soil Physics Dictate Foundation Design?</h2>
<p>Western India presents three dominant soil regimes. In Gujarat, black cotton clay expands up to 12 % when wet and shrinks 8 % on drying. In Goa, laterite contains 45‑50 % iron oxide and aluminum silicates; it hardens after 60‑90 days of air‑drying. In the semi‑arid zones of Maharashtra, lateritic sand‑clay mixes exhibit a 30 % sand, 55 % silt, 15 % clay composition. These numbers drive foundation depth. I dig to the depth where the moisture content stabilises below 12 % – 1.5 m for black cotton clay, 0.8 m for laterite, 1.0 m for mixed sand‑clay.</p>
<p>Field test protocol: Fill a 20‑litre bucket with excavated soil, let settle for 24 hours, then measure layer thickness. Ideal profile: 15‑30 % clay, 50‑70 % sand. Next, roll a 15 mm ribbon of moist soil; if it bends without cracking, the soil passes the sausage test. Finally, perform the knuckle‑dent test on a fresh lime‑plaster sample; a smooth, non‑powdery surface indicates proper carbonation potential.</p>
<p>These diagnostics replace the generic “soil is good” claim. On the Odiyar Farm project, the jar test revealed 22 % clay, prompting a 1.2 m deep footing with 8 % hydraulic lime. The result was zero settlement after two monsoons.</p>
